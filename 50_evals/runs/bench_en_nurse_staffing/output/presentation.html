Move to unit-based staffing to keep new nurses and cut agency spend

Decision for the Executive Leadership Team
November 2026 · Dr. Lena Okafor, Chief Nursing Officer

Every nursing unit follows one monthly rotation today.

How nurses are assigned across Northvale’s units since January 2024
WHAT CHANGED IN 2024
All 1,800 nurses now move to a different unit every month, whatever that unit’s care model.
Source: Northvale Health nursing workforce policy, January 2024. 1

Outpatient nurses see more patients when teams stay together.

Fixed-team clinics saw 18.6 patients per nurse shift versus 15.1 under rotation.
KEY IMPLICATION & ACTION
Keep outpatient nurses on stable home teams.

Unit-based staffing would save about $4.3M a year.

Modeled annual savings from unit-based staffing ($M)

KEY IMPLICATION & ACTION
Book the agency savings in the FY2026 nursing budget.

Weekend coverage holds steady under unit-based staffing.

Three units
Open weekend shifts stay flat or fall by one in every unit; Emergency stays the hardest to fill.
KEY IMPLICATION & ACTION
Keep the weekend float pool for Emergency only.
Source: Northvale operations scheduling model, FY2026. 6

Approve unit-based staffing to stop the turnover and agency drain.

Decision requested and first three steps
PRIMARY ACTION REQUESTED TODAY
Approve unit-based staffing from next quarter (fallback: a six-month pilot in Critical Care and Emergency).

Step 1: Home units

Assign every nurse a home unit; new graduates stay put for two years.

Step 2: Planned cross-training

Two scheduled cross-unit shifts per quarter for experienced nurses.

Step 3: Track the savings

Report agency spend and first-year turnover monthly against the $4.3M target.

Source: Northvale nursing workforce review, FY2026. 7
