# `LANG-EN` — English Slide Typography and Takeaway Title Rules

## 1. Statement
When authoring presentation artifacts (`context_brief.md`, `storyline.md`, `slide_spec.yaml`) in English (`en`), enforce crisp active-voice syntax, Sentence case capitalization, strict word-count ceilings for `Takeaway Titles` (target 5–10 words, hard ceiling 12 words), and standardized English numeric/currency formatting.

## 2. Why It Matters
English business slides frequently suffer from nominalizations (*"The implementation of..."*), Title Case headers that slow down reading speed, and run-on 25-word sentences generated by LLMs. Enforcing tight lexical and typographic constraints ensures English slides scan effortlessly in the upper-left Z-pattern.

## 3. Agent Application
- **`Takeaway Title` Syntax & Length:**
  - Target **5–10 words** (hard maximum: **12 words** / ~75 characters so it fits on a single line at 20–24pt font).
  - Use **Sentence case** (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns; do not use All-Words Title Case).
  - Use an **active finite verb** in the present or future tense (*"cuts"*, *"recaptures"*, *"hinders"*, *"approve"*); avoid weak passive constructions (*"is impacted by"*) and noun-heavy openers (*"An analysis of..."*).
- **`Descriptive Chart Title` & Axis Labels:**
  - Use Sentence case noun phrases without terminal periods (e.g., *"Customer service calls per minute"* or *"First-year turnover (%) by tenure cohort, 2023–2025"*).
- **Association Wording for Observational Results (`D-040`):** When the brief's `study_design` is observational or descriptive, titles, callouts and notes state associations (*"is higher among"*, *"is associated with"*, *"differs by"*), not causes. Avoid *causes, drives, leads to, results in, triggers, compounds, elevates, boosts, motivates, due to, because of, impact on*, and *effect* in chart, panel and axis titles (*marginal effects* is a name, not a claim). Name the group the estimate covers (*"renters and mortgagors"*, not *"Australians"*). A coefficient below its reference group is *"less likely than mortgagors"*, not *"oppose"* or *"resist"*, unless the source shows a majority against (`D-043`).
- **Number, Currency & Unit Formatting:**
  - Use `,` as the thousands separator and `.` as the decimal separator (`1,250`, `$5.6M`, `38.5%`).
  - Strip uninformative trailing decimal zeros on axis labels (`$5M` instead of `$5.00M`; `40%` instead of `40.0%`).
  - Abbreviate large magnitudes consistently (`K`, `M`, `B`).

## 4. Testable Checks
- **Word Count Gate:** `takeaway_title` word count is between 4 and 12 words (`target <= 10`).
- **Sentence Case Check:** `takeaway_title`, `chart_title`, and `axis_title` use Sentence case rather than Title Case or ALL CAPS.
- **No Nominalization Openers:** `takeaway_title` does not begin with *"Overview of"*, *"Analysis of"*, *"Summary of"*, or *"Comparison of"*.
- **Consistent Numeric Separators:** All English numbers use comma thousands separators and period decimals with zero redundant trailing zeros on axes.
- **Causal-Verb Lint (`D-040`, `D-043`):** For observational or descriptive designs the validator warns on the causal verbs above in titles, callouts, annotations, steps and notes, and on *effect* in chart, panel and axis titles. On research decks it warns on *oppose, resist, reject* beside model estimates.
- **Findings, Not Instructions or Verdicts (`D-043`, `D-046`):** When the brief's objective is `shared_understanding`, callouts, annotations and steps state what the findings mean; the validator warns on *must, should, need to, requires*, and in titles too on verdicts about how others frame the issue (*misdirects, misleads, misguided, the wrong axis*). Absence words (*only, no, not*) need every excluded estimate clearly non-significant, and *highest/lowest* needs an interval that clears the next one (`D-046`).
- **Stated Quantities (`D-043`):** A share or percentage written in words (*"two-fifths"*, *"three in ten"*, *"28%"*) must match a number plotted on that slide within ±3 points (or ±10% of the stated value); the validator warns otherwise.

## 5. Good vs. Bad Example
- **Bad (`LANG-EN` Violations):** *"An Analysis Of Why Uniform Nurse Rotation Is Negatively Impacting Our New Graduate Retention Rates By 13.00% ($1,700,000.00 Cost)"* *(19 words, Title Case, nominalized opener, trailing zeros).*
- **Good (`LANG-EN` Compliant):**
  - `Takeaway Title`: *"First-year turnover nearly doubled in Critical Care after rotation."* *(9 words, Sentence case, active verb).*
  - `Descriptive Chart Title`: *"First-year turnover (%) by unit and tenure, 2023 (before rotation) vs. 2025"*
