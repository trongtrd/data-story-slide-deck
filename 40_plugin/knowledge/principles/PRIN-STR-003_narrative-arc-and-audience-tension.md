# `PRIN-STR-003` — Structure Content Around the Narrative Arc and Audience Tension

## 1. Statement
Organize presentations around a **Narrative Arc** (`Plot` → `Rising Action` → `Climax` → `Falling Action` → `Resolution`, or the compact three-part **Story Mountain**: `Plot` → `Twist` → `Ending`) driven by `Narrative Tension`—the gap that matters to the `Audience` between current reality/risk and their desired outcome. Select the `Climax` (human impact vs. financial impact) based on what creates peak urgency for the primary decision-makers in the room.

## 2. Why It Matters
Without `Narrative Tension`, a presentation reads like a flat list of facts that audiences quickly forget and feel no urgency to act upon. Structuring evidence so tension rises to an undeniable peak (`Climax`)—whether that peak is human pain (new hires struggling and quitting) or financial bleed—makes maintaining the status quo uncomfortable and turns the `Primary Action` into the natural relief.

## 3. Agent Application
- In `storyline-builder` (`Checkpoint G2`), assign every slide in `storyline.md` to an explicit narrative stage:
  1. **Plot:** Shared context, common ground, and credible scope.
  2. **Rising Action:** Accumulating empirical evidence that the current reality is failing or creating hidden risk/cost.
  3. **Climax:** The single sharpest moment of tension (human or financial cost) tailored to the primary champion's values in `context_brief.md`.
  4. **Falling Action:** Root-cause synthesis, quantified upside of the solution, and preemptive objection handling.
  5. **Resolution:** Explicit `Primary Action` and immediate next steps.
- Do not default blindly to putting the biggest dollar figure at the `Climax`; check `context_brief.md` to decide whether human/organizational impact or financial impact hits the primary decision-maker harder, using the other as reinforcing evidence in `Falling Action`.
- Plan explicit narrative bridges wherever the storyline shifts domains (e.g., bridging from agency spend to nurse retention).

## 4. Testable Checks
- **Explicit Arc Mapping:** Every slide in `storyline.md` is tagged with one of the 5 `Narrative Arc` stages (or 3 `Story Mountain` stages for short 3–5 slide decks).
- **Identifiable Climax:** Exactly 1–2 slides are designated as the `Climax`, stating the highest-urgency problem or cost before transitioning to the solution.
- **Domain Bridge Presence:** Whenever adjacent slides switch analytical domains (e.g., agency cost → HR turnover), `storyline.md` includes a transition note explaining the logical link.

## 5. Good vs. Bad Example
- **Bad (Flat Fact List):** Slide 1: Survey results. Slide 2: Roster data. Slide 3: Competency scores. Slide 4: Turnover numbers. Slide 5: Agency costs. Slide 6: Recommendations.
- **Good (Tension-Driven Narrative Arc):**
  - *Plot (Slide 1):* Every nursing unit follows one monthly rotation.
  - *Rising Action (Slide 2):* Outpatient teams that stay together see more patients.
  - *Climax (Slides 3–4):* The sharpest cost is human—new graduates lose competence and first-year turnover nearly doubles.
  - *Falling Action (Slides 5–6):* Unit-based staffing saves about $4.3M a year while weekend coverage holds steady.
  - *Resolution (Slide 7):* Approve unit-based staffing today to stop the drain.
