# `PRIN-CTX-004` — Reframe Operational Metrics Into Audience-Specific Strategic Stakes

## 1. Statement
Raw metric changes do not speak for themselves; communicators must translate operational metrics into concrete `Stakes`—the specific strategic benefits if the `Audience` takes the `Primary Action` and the tangible risks (financial, operational, or institutional) if they do not.

## 2. Why It Matters
An analyst may celebrate that an operational metric (such as inventory turnover or bed occupancy) crossed a multi-year threshold, while executives remain unmoved because they do not immediately connect that metric to capital efficiency, competitive positioning, or talent retention. Articulating both positive benefits and downside risks—including the institutional risk of ignoring commissioned evidence—creates the urgency required to overcome status-quo inertia.

## 3. Agent Application
- When reviewing input metrics during `Checkpoint G1`, ask: *"Why does this number matter to the specific decision-makers in the room?"*
- Translate abstract or operational findings into concrete stakeholder outcomes:
  - **Benefits of acting:** e.g., freeing working capital, reducing agency-staffing costs, reversing first-year turnover, accelerating onboarding.
  - **Risks of inaction:** e.g., continued cash lock-up in obsolete stock, paying for empty desks, losing high-performing specialists, and eroding organizational trust in data-driven decision-making.
- Reject vague buzzwords ("better outcomes", "higher efficiency", "synergies") when drafting `Stakes` in `context_brief.md`.

## 4. Testable Checks
- **Dual-Sided Stakes:** `context_brief.md` explicitly specifies both (a) concrete benefits of acting and (b) concrete risks of not acting.
- **Zero Buzzword Padding:** Neither `Stakes` nor the `Big Idea` relies on unquantified clichés ("better outcomes", "improved synergy", "value optimization") without naming the specific business lever (cost, retention, revenue, risk, speed).
- **Stakeholder Tie-Back:** Each primary stakeholder priority identified in `PRIN-CTX-002` maps to at least one articulated benefit or risk in `Stakes`.

## 5. Good vs. Bad Example
- **Bad:** "Inventory turnover reached 10.7 vs. the industry benchmark of 10.2, which will drive better operational outcomes for the company."
- **Good:** "Pushing inventory turnover above the industry benchmark for the first time in six years frees up working capital, lowers holding costs, and gives us supply-chain agility to fund Q3 growth initiatives—whereas slipping back risks tying up cash in obsolete stock and losing our competitive edge."
